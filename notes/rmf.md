
# 🧠 Risk Management Framework (RMF) – NIST

The **Risk Management Framework (RMF)** is a structured process developed by NIST to help organizations manage and mitigate security and privacy risks. As an entry-level security analyst, understanding these steps helps you support organizational security goals effectively.

---

## 🔹 Step 1: **Prepare**
> _Set the foundation for managing risks before incidents occur._

- Monitor for early signs of risk.
- Identify possible controls to reduce risks.
- Support your team in organizing security tools, playbooks, and documentation.

---

## 🔹 Step 2: **Categorize**
> _Assess what types of data and systems you're protecting._

- Follow protocols that classify data based on confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA Triad).
- Understand how risk impacts critical assets like customer data.
- Execute risk-reduction processes designed by your organization.

---

## 🔹 Step 3: **Select**
> _Choose and document security controls to protect the organization._

- Keep playbooks and documentation current.
- Help maintain a list of approved tools, policies, and countermeasures.
- Provide input based on observed issues in the environment.

---

## 🔹 Step 4: **Implement**
> _Put controls into action and update security configurations._

- Help roll out password policy changes, MFA, or secure file-sharing tools.
- Support team efforts to apply patches or system hardening based on risks.
- Identify patterns (e.g., repeated password resets) and recommend solutions.

---

## 🔹 Step 5: **Assess**
> _Evaluate how well the controls are working._

- Analyze logs and SIEM alerts to check for misconfigurations or gaps.
- Test tools or scripts to see if they’re meeting goals.
- Flag weaknesses or recommend updates to improve performance.

---

## 🔹 Step 6: **Authorize**
> _Document and take ownership of risk decisions._

- Help generate risk assessment reports.
- Support plan-of-action documents and timelines for remediation.
- Ensure security actions align with business goals.

---

## 🔹 Step 7: **Monitor**
> _Continuously review and improve system performance and security posture._

- Track system performance using monitoring tools (e.g., Wazuh, Splunk).
- Watch for unusual behavior in logs or traffic.
- Suggest improvements if systems no longer meet security goals.
