# Security Audits

A **security audit** is a review of an organization's security controls, policies, and procedures against internal and external expectations. It is used to evaluate whether an organization meets required standards and complies with laws and regulations.

---

## 🔍 Purpose of a Security Audit

- Evaluate controls and procedures
- Maintain security posture
- Identify threats, risks, and vulnerabilities
- Ensure remediation processes are in place

---

## 🎯 Goals and Objectives

- **Goal**: Ensure IT practices align with industry and organizational standards
- **Objective**: Identify failures, recommend fixes, and improve overall security maturity

---

## 🧩 Factors That Affect Audits

- Industry type
- Organization size
- Government regulations
- Business geography
- Voluntary compliance with standards

---

## 🧱 Frameworks & Controls in Audits

- **Frameworks** help prepare for regulatory audits  
  Examples:
  - [NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
  - [ISO 27000 Series](https://www.iso.org/isoiec-27001-information-security.html)

- **Controls** support alignment with frameworks:
  - Administrative / Managerial
  - Technical
  - Physical

---

## 📝 Audit Checklist

### 1. Identify the Scope
- What assets are being assessed (e.g. firewalls, PII, physical access)?
- What are the audit goals?
- How often will the audit be performed?

### 2. Evaluate Policies & Procedures
- Ensure processes are functioning and being followed

### 3. Complete a Risk Assessment
- Analyze risks related to:
  - Budget
  - Controls
  - Internal processes
  - External regulations

### 4. Conduct the Audit
- Assess the security posture of listed assets

### 5. Create a Mitigation Plan
- Strategy to reduce risk and potential negative impacts

### 6. Communicate Results
- Deliver a report with:
  - Findings
  - Recommendations
  - Compliance gaps
