# 🧱 The Relationship Between Security Frameworks and Controls

## 🔹 What Are Frameworks?
Security frameworks are structured guidelines that help organizations:
- Develop security policies and procedures
- Comply with legal and regulatory standards (e.g., HIPAA)
- Reduce overall risk exposure

> 🏥 **Example:** A hospital uses a framework to align with HIPAA.

---

## 🔹 What Are Controls?
Security controls are specific actions, technologies, or policies used to:
- Prevent, detect, or correct security issues
- Support the framework in mitigating threats, risks, and vulnerabilities

> 🔐 **Example:** Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is a technical control that helps protect patient data.

---

## 🛠 Types of Controls:
| Type              | Examples                                                                 |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Physical**       | Gates, locks, CCTV, motion sensors, ID cards                             |
| **Technical**      | MFA, firewalls, antivirus software                                       |
| **Administrative** | Authorization protocols, separation of duties, asset classification      |

---

## 📚 Common Frameworks:
| Framework | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **CTF (Cyber Threat Framework)** | Developed by U.S. government to create a common language for threat analysis |
| **ISO/IEC 27001** | International standard for information security management systems (ISMS). Offers best practices and control options but does not mandate specific controls. |

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaways:
- Frameworks = **Strategic guidance**  
- Controls = **Tactical execution**
- Used together, they help organizations meet compliance requirements and strengthen cybersecurity posture.
